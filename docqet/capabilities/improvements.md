It answers one question for anyone looking at the software: what would make
this more ready? It's asked of whatever they're looking at, whether that's
one capability, a feature, an area, a project or the whole product, what
serves a persona, or an initiative, and the improvements count the same
capabilities its readiness does.

It replaces what was shown as what's left everywhere: a list of every open
gap and decision says what's open, not what would help, and what's left
read as a finish line. Every open gap and decision is still listed, under
the improvements.

It's not a finish line or a to-do list. Software that keeps growing is never
done, and readiness is a recommendation that people judge. Improvements say
what could be done and what each would change. Whether any of it is worth
doing, and when to stop, is for people to decide.

## Includes

- Everyone sees, for each improvement, how much it would raise readiness,
  and whether it does that by lowering exposure or raising maturity.
- Promoting a capability to its next level is an improvement, raising
  maturity.
- Promoting every capability in a feature or an area to its next level is
  one improvement too, where that's more than one capability.
- Improvements are shown by part, one level below what's being looked at:
  a product's or project's areas; an area's, a persona's or an
  initiative's features; a feature's capabilities; and a capability's own
  steps. Where everything falls in one part, they're the next level down.
  One capability among many moves readiness little, so this shows where
  the larger steps are, and what holds readiness where it is.
- A gap or decision that's the riskiest on capabilities in more than one
  part is a part of its own, so it isn't hidden in any one.
- Each part shows its potential: how ready what's being looked at could be
  with all of the part settled, every capability in it at GA with nothing
  open on it. Parts are ordered by potential, most first. Potentials
  overlap, since a capability can be in more than one part, so they don't
  add up.
- Each part shows its next step too: the one improvement in it that would
  raise readiness most, promoting all of it a level among them, and where
  readiness would be after it. Where no one step raises it, it says so.
- Each part's readiness now, where its next step would take it, and its
  potential are drawn together, so how far each could go shows at a
  glance, and pointing at a part shows the change its next step would
  make.
- Opening a part shows its own improvements, by part one level down, so
  anyone can follow what holds readiness down to the steps that would
  raise it.
- Everyone can also see every improvement one at a time, each a single
  step, most first.
- Closing the riskiest gap on a capability is an improvement, lowering
  exposure by the difference between it and the next riskiest gap or
  decision.
- Answering the riskiest rated open decision on a capability is an
  improvement too, lowering exposure the same way. It says what's at stake
  in the answer changing what people rely on, not something falling short,
  and what the answer becomes, such as a new gap, would count on its own. A
  decision with no ratings raises nothing, so it's never an improvement.
- A gap or decision that isn't the riskiest on any capability raises
  nothing yet, so it isn't listed until it is.
- One gap or decision that's the riskiest on several capabilities, such as
  one on a mechanism they're built on, or a decision on a feature or
  area, is one improvement, counting all of them.
- Each improvement says when it would move readiness into the next
  quarter, such as from Partial to Viable.
- In a list of capabilities, features or areas, each shows the next
  step that would raise its readiness most, and how many more parts there
  are.
- With one initiative showing, improvements count only what's in it, as
  every other figure does.
- Something with nothing that would raise its readiness, such as a feature
  whose capabilities are all GA with no gaps, shows no improvements, without
  saying it's done.
